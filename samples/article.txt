AI is now responsible for a huge and rapidly growing share of all web traffic — some measurements put automated traffic above 50%, others closer to 35%, but every major report agrees it's climbing fast. This isn't just old-school search engine crawlers anymore; it's AI companies scraping the web to train and power chatbots, plus a new wave of autonomous AI agents that browse, click, and complete tasks on people's behalf. The catch is that many AI crawlers read huge numbers of pages from a site while sending very little traffic back, breaking the old deal where Google crawled you and paid you back in readers. This is squeezing publishers, making bot detection much harder since AI-driven bots now behave a lot like real humans, and quietly polluting analytics that weren't built to separate bot activity from real visitors. The bottom line: the internet's traffic is shifting from being mostly human to being substantially machine, and the businesses that adapt their content, security, and measurement strategies fastest will be the ones that come out ahead.
